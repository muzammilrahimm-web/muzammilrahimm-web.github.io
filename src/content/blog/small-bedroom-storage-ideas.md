A small bedroom can feel calm and restful, as long as everything in it has a home. The trick is not to squeeze in more furniture, but to use the space you already have more cleverly: under the bed, up the walls and behind doors.

Here are the ideas we come back to again and again when a bedroom feels too full.

## Start with a quick reset

Before you buy a single storage box, spend twenty minutes clearing surfaces. Take everything off your bedside table, dresser and the floor around the bed, then put back only what truly belongs in a bedroom.

Anything that lives elsewhere (mugs, paperwork, the laundry that never made it to the wardrobe) goes back to its proper room. You will often find the bedroom needs less storage than you thought.

## Use the space under the bed

The area under a bed is one of the largest storage spaces in a small home, and it is usually wasted.

### Choose the right containers

- **Lidded boxes** keep dust off spare bedding and out-of-season clothes.
- **Drawers on castors** are easier to pull out than boxes, which makes them better for things you use every week.
- **Vacuum bags** work well for bulky duvets and winter coats, but avoid them for natural fibres you want to keep fluffy.

### Consider a storage bed

If you are replacing your bed anyway, a frame with built-in drawers or a lift-up ottoman base can hold as much as a small chest of drawers, without taking up any extra floor space.

## Make the bedside work harder

A bedside table only needs to hold what you use at night. Pick a style with a drawer or a lower shelf so books, chargers and hand cream can be tucked away.

If floor space is very tight, try a **wall-mounted shelf** or a small floating drawer instead of a table. Pair it with a wall light or a plug-in sconce to free up the surface completely.

## Go vertical

When the floor is full, look up.

- A **tall, narrow chest of drawers** stores more than a wide, low one in the same footprint.
- **Shelves above the door** are perfect for items you rarely need, such as spare pillows or keepsake boxes.
- **Hooks behind the door** hold dressing gowns, tomorrow's outfit or a bag you use every day.

Keep higher shelves calm by using matching baskets, so the room does not feel top-heavy.

## Add a bench at the foot of the bed

A bench or a pair of upholstered stools gives you somewhere to sit while you get dressed, and a place to fold throws and extra cushions at night. Choose one with a lift-up lid or a shelf underneath and it becomes useful storage too.

## Build small daily habits

Storage only works if it is easy to keep up. Two habits make the biggest difference:

1. **Make the bed every morning.** It takes two minutes and instantly makes the whole room look tidier.
2. **Do a five-minute reset at night.** Return clothes to the wardrobe, clear the bedside and put away anything that wandered in during the day.

## A calm room, one step at a time

You do not need to do all of this in one weekend. Start with the space under the bed, then tackle the bedside, then the walls. Each small change makes your bedroom feel a little larger and a lot more restful.
