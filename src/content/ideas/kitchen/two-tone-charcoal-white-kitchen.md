Two-tone kitchens are a smart way to add colour without overwhelming a room. Here, **charcoal base cabinets** are grounded and practical, while **white subway tiles** and pale walls keep the space bright.

## The look

The darker colour sits low, where it hides everyday scuffs and makes the kitchen feel anchored. Above, glossy white tiles reflect light around the room. **Open oak shelves** bring in natural warmth and break up the wall, and a few brass details add a subtle, elegant finish.

## Why it works

- **Balanced contrast.** Dark below and light above is a classic combination that always looks considered.
- **Practical colour.** Deeper shades on base units are more forgiving of marks.
- **Flexible style.** Swap brass for black hardware for a more industrial feel, or for wood for a softer look.

## Colour combinations to try

- **Charcoal + white + oak**: timeless and warm (shown here)
- **Sage green + cream + brass**: soft and welcoming
- **Navy + white + walnut**: crisp, with a coastal feel

## How to recreate it

1. Paint or replace **only the lower cabinet doors**. It's a cost-effective update.
2. Choose a **light, simple tile** for the walls if you're retiling.
3. Add **one or two open shelves** for everyday cups and bowls.
4. Repeat your metal finish (brass, black or chrome) at least three times so it feels intentional.
