Living in one open room doesn't mean everything has to blur together. With the right **furniture arrangement**, a studio or open-plan apartment can feel like a home with distinct, comfortable zones.

## The look

A **tall white cube shelving unit** acts as both storage and a backdrop, filled with books, records and plants. In front of it, a **compact wooden dining table** with mid-century chairs creates a clear dining area. A green sofa with its back to the table marks the start of the living zone.

## The furniture arrangement

- **Living zone:** sofa, coffee table and rug, facing the window or TV.
- **Dining zone:** a table close to the kitchen, lit by its own pendant.
- **Storage wall:** one large unit instead of many small ones keeps the room calmer.

Placing the sofa with its back to the dining table is a simple trick: it creates an invisible wall without blocking light or views.

## How to recreate it

1. **Decide on your zones** before buying furniture: usually living, dining, working and sleeping.
2. **Use rugs and lighting** to define each one.
3. **Choose one tall storage piece** that can also divide the space.
4. **Float furniture away from the walls** where possible. It sounds counter-intuitive, but it creates clearer areas.

The result is an apartment that feels organized, practical and much larger than its floor plan.
